# Subjects and composition — what to show and how to stage it

## 1. Composition rules for every scene

1. **One bold subject** readable as a silhouette in 0.25 s at thumbnail size (120 px).
   Reduce forms to straights and curves; no interior modelling; depth comes from overlap,
   size and value, never from shading. "Count the wings, not the feathers."
2. **Value first, colour second.** Decide which plate is darkest, which is mid, which is
   the bright accent before picking hues. The darkest ink carries line and shadow, the mid
   ink carries supporting shapes, the brightest ink carries the focal point.
3. **The anchor lands on meaning.** The motif's fixed frame position must coincide with the
   scene's most meaningful point: a bell body, a record spindle, a flower heart, the place
   a howl starts. Write it in the entry.
4. **Rhyme with the motif.** Most scenes contain a concentric or radial element that
   echoes the motif: sound arcs, water rings, light beams, sunbursts, phone dials.
5. **3–4 inks, roles assigned**, knockouts listed, misregistration direction fixed.
6. **Strong value contrast** between subject and ground. If the subject is light, the ground
   plate is dark with a knockout; if the subject is dark, the ground is paper or a light tint.
7. **Texture last.** Grain, misregistration and voids are consequences, not the design. A
   scene must read with all texture removed.

## 2. Subject library, scored for the medium

Score reflects how naturally the subject becomes a silhouette plus a radial rhyme in
3–4 inks. Use HIGH freely, MEDIUM with a stated plan, LOW only with a written reason.

### HIGH — radial and concentric by nature
bell with sound arcs · ripples on a pond · full moon and phases · sunburst and sunrise
behind ridges · vinyl record and spindle · rotary telephone dial · radio with sound arcs
· ringed planet · target, eye, iris · sunflower heart · lighthouse beam · fireworks burst
· campfire glow · spiral galaxy under a radio telescope · Ferris wheel · umbrella, parasol
· bicycle wheel · clock face · lotus and daisy blooms · raindrop rings

### HIGH — animals in profile
howling wolf · whale and its shafts of light · owl close-up · cat from behind at a window
· cricket, grasshopper · heron, crane · koi · jellyfish · bat flock · fox · hummingbird
at a flower · frog on a lily pad · swallow, crow, goose in a V

### HIGH — light emitters and weather
aurora over snow · lightning over a volcano · lantern, candle, match · stars and star
grain · fog and rain as grain plates · shafts of light in water or forest

### MEDIUM — machines and objects with one iconic outline
kettle on a tiled wall · rocket on a gantry · hot-air balloon · sailing boat · piano or
harp strings · valve radio cabinet · teapot · lighthouse · windmill · tram or train head-on
· telescope · typewriter (avoid its keys' text)

### MEDIUM — layered landscapes
mountain ridges at sunrise · furrowed fields to a horizon · dunes · terraced hills ·
harbour with masts · apartment windows at night · sunflower field · jungle waterfall ·
breaking wave · umbrella pine · reeds and moon

### LOW — needs a reason
human faces with expression · crowds · cluttered interiors · glass, chrome, water
reflections · realistic skin and fur · fine text, signage, screens · subtle sunsets
with no shapes · anything whose identity depends on small detail

## 3. Story types and how they map

| story register | motif candidates | anchor | typical acts |
|---|---|---|---|
| call and response, signals, echoes | dot + ripple; bell + arcs; lighthouse + beam | frame centre | call → world answers → memory fades → answer → together |
| memory, nostalgia, fading | photo medallions on orbits; ink drain to one colour | centre | present → archive → drain → return |
| nature's rhythms | moon phases; tide rings; migrating V | centre or upper third | cycle through seasons or hours on the beat grid |
| music, sound, radio | record spindle; dial; sound arcs | centre | tune-in → montage on beats → silence → signature |
| fable, bedtime story | a lantern, a small animal in profile | lower third | journey through discs → home |
| one-concept explainer | the concept as a single geometric figure | centre | figure → variations → reprint in two inks |
| poster, festival, greeting | sunburst; flower bloom | centre | reveal plate by plate → hold → lettering |

## 4. Scene entry, worked example

```
### 12. Temple bell ringing         t = 4.5–6.0 s, opens as a disc on the anchor, dives to full bleed at 4.9
- Silhouette: one bell profile, twice as tall as wide, shoulder at 1/5, slight waist, flared lip; beam above, striker log right.
- Anchor: the bell body. The motif dot sits on the bell's belly and the sound arcs radiate from it.
- Inks: violet = base (bell body) · yellow = overprint (radial glow, dot-size ramp from centre, 0° screen) · orange = accent (beam, striker) · blue = line (ropes, bands, arcs, 45°). Misregistration: violet (0,0), yellow (+2,+2), blue (−2,+1), orange (+1,−1).
- Knockouts: lip gleam and bosses are paper-coloured marks over violet; the glow prints under the bell (violet over yellow dots reads as print texture, allowed).
- Tone: glow = dot ramp, pitch 9 px; bell shading = blue dots down the right side, 15°; solids for beam and bell.
- Motif rhyme: three sound arcs left and right, radii 190/240/290, pulse on each strike.
- Live element: striker swings in 8 frames, arcs scale 0.9→1.14 and fade over 26 frames, three strikes on the beat.
- Don't: no gradient on the bell; no outline in a second ink.
```
